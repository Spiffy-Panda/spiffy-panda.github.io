Deep dive · instrument design & validation

Synthetic users, treated as an instrument that has to earn its readings

Six conditions, three hypotheses with their nulls written first, and an apparatus that proves comparability rather than assuming it. 51 traces captured, 30 of them coded — the grounding measure scored against a scheme fixed before any coding began. The method, the three defects the runs found, and why the answer right now is stop running.

01Where synthetic users are valid

They pilot instruments and pressure-test designs. They never replace participants and never support a frequency claim.

A seven-person corpus can find attitudinal patterns. It cannot pressure-test a product against those patterns fifty times. Synthetic users close that gap for exactly three jobs — piloting an instrument before it touches a human, pressure-testing a design against a known attitude, and generating hypotheses worth asking a human about — and for nothing else. Not participant replacement, not prevalence, not a stand-in for a population segment.

The known failure mode is flattening: an ungrounded LLM user converges toward one agreeable, competent, unbothered person regardless of the mask. That is what I set out to measure rather than assume. The carrier design — attitudinal spectrums, no demographics, shift triggers, and a task payload fixed outside the mask — is in §04 of the project write-up and not repeated here.

Two terms recur below. A stop line (“canary”) is an exact sentence a condition must say to end a session — because it is exact, a paraphrase is detectable drift, which makes it the only fidelity check the scaffold-only control has. Anchor echo is whether a carrier's behaviour matches the interview-derived observables listed for it: the grounding test itself.

02Hypotheses, with the nulls written first

Each hypothesis has a pre-written outcome that would kill the project. That is the point of them.

HypothesisWhat the null would mean
H1Grounded conditions diverge from the scaffold-only control on the probe events If control ≈ grounded: the scaffold and model default already produce the behaviour. Grounding is decorative — stop investing in it.
H2Grounded conditions diverge from the ungrounded arm, and track their interview anchors where it tracks generic priors If ungrounded ≈ grounded on every measure: a model can invent the mindset without the interviews. Grounding buys legitimacy, not behaviour — weigh that before funding another derivation.
H3The grounded carriers stay separable from each other under real product pressure If they collapse together: the mindset layer is too weak for apparatus use; sharpen the contrasts.

Predictions exist only for the grounded carriers. The control and ungrounded cells are deliberately unscripted — record what happens, because a prediction written for a baseline is a prediction the baseline can be marked against unfairly.

Planning or backstop language in a WREN trace is leakage from the older carrier, and the batch gets flagged.

Provenance, stated precisely

No verbatim transcript quote backs any of these prompts. The live wave is derived from a teammate's secondary synthesis of the full seven-participant corpus — not from raw transcripts and not from my three-lens coding pass, which covered only the first three — and every behavioural rule traces to a stated attribute pole or a named behavioural-logic sentence in that synthesis. So the carriers are two steps from the interviews, and two of the four are anchored partly on participants whose transcripts are not in my workspace at all. The ungrounded arm's authorship property, by contrast, is protected mechanically: a fresh, siloed agent with no repo or evidence access cannot have absorbed the grounding it is supposed to lack.

04Apparatus

One frozen product image per arm, rebuilt per trial, with byte-identity of the starting artifact proved by a three-point hash chain rather than assumed.

The product under test is a standalone, agent-native AI coding desktop app — not an editor extension, not a web product, not a CLI. The distinction is load-bearing: the surface has no editor and no terminal of its own, which is exactly why it is worth pointing at people who do not write code. Each arm gets one image, pinned by digest, rebuilt per trial — not a shared VM, which accumulates state across conditions and makes runs non-comparable. The digest goes in the batch memo, because the image is the product-version stamp.

The seeded artifact, and why byte-identity is proved rather than trusted

Two task cards start from an artifact the user “built earlier” — a small events page, stale for one card and working for the other, identical for every condition. It is placed as a per-run parameter, never baked into the image, because re-committing the image would change the digest every manifest records as the product version.

The state is hashed on the host, checked against a recorded hash, copied in through a read-only bind mount, re-hashed inside the container, and re-hashed again at run start. Every condition's manifest must show the same seed tree hash — that is the proof the cells are comparable, and it sits in the memo beside the image digest. A run on either card without a seed argument aborts before spending, and the seed state is cross-checked against the card, because swapping the two would silently measure the wrong thing.

One harness rule is measurement rather than plumbing: the generated prompt replaces the harness system prompt rather than being appended to it. Appending would leave each condition running a slightly different scaffold, and H1 — entirely a claim about what the shared scaffold alone produces — would stop meaning anything. For the same reason stop lines are never re-asserted between turns: they are canaries, and repeating one destroys it as a measure.

05What gets measured

Six measures, three of them auto-captured and three coded by hand — and the grounding claim rests entirely on the coded ones.

#MeasureCapture
1Stop-line discipline — the exact line, at the right moment, no paraphraseAuto
2Probe events — behaviour at a permission ask, polished-looking output, a recurring failure, a jargon wallCoded
3Vocabulary + off-character behaviour — per-condition word lists, plus pre-registered surfaces the mindset would not open and moves it would not makeMixed
4Tension appearances — do grounded carriers surface their held contradictions? Counted and quotedCoded
5Trajectory stats — turns to done or quit, which canary fired, obstacles abandonedAuto
6Anchor echo — the grounding testCoded

Measure 3's behavioural half was added after a batch produced a signal the word detector could not read: two conditions spent much of several traces outside the app entirely, while the banned-word list covered that point for only two of five conditions. The control has neither list by design — its drift toward engineer-fluent speech is the H1 baseline, not a violation. Anchor-echo scores are also never pooled into a composite: a single grounding number would hide exactly the per-attribute pattern the study exists to see, and one of the five attributes is a manipulation check rather than grounding evidence at all.

06Pre-registration, and who actually coded

The measure with a pre-written rubric agreed 60% better than the one without — but the raters are LLM sessions, so this is codeability evidence, not human reliability.

The gap is the rubric — same traces, same two lenses, same day. But my own protocol is explicit that a measure with no pre-registered rubric cannot earn reliability evidence: the 0.417 is a codeability diagnostic, never a reliability warrant. It says the codebook was underspecified there, and the disagreements confirm it — they cluster on three undefined boundaries, each closable with one sentence of rule.

Reported as the protocol requires: κ never bare, always with its n, po, pe, both marginals and the full confusion matrix, and never a verbal benchmark like “substantial.” Where a rater used a value the codebook does not define, or the notes do not carry the unit the pre-registration specifies, the calculator runs both defensible treatments and reports the band rather than picking one — improvising a unit is the failure that tool exists to prevent.

Who the raters are

Two analytical lenses — one HCDE, one CS/AI — each run as a fresh, siloed LLM session per trace, applying one codebook. Not two people, and not one session doing both. I designed it this way to get agreement evidence at a scale one student cannot staff, and the same siloed-multi-lens pattern runs through the interview coding. What it tests is whether my codebook is specific enough to survive a different reader. What it cannot test is whether the codes match human judgement — there is no human reliability check anywhere in this project, and that is the next thing worth spending money on.

The order things were fixed in

  1. Before any coding — 0 of 51 traces coded The anchor-echo scoring scheme is fixed and published: an observable→attribute codebook, 18 ordinal pairs, five condition-neutral indices. Scoring is against the frozen condition files, never against the derivation source's own rating table. binding
  2. Same window, still 0 of 51 coded The rater design is fixed: which six traces are double-coded, by what rule, on which two lenses, and how disagreements are handled. binding
  3. After the six, before the other 24 The frozen clause list is published — 35 clauses for the grounded carriers plus 10 for the ungrounded arm — fixing the κ denominators. It lands after the double-coded six, which is why every Measure 6 number carries the amendment stamp rather than a clean pre-registration claim. amends the scheme mid-stream
  4. Mid-batch Rubrics for probe events, tensions, the outcome key and the H1/H2/H3 decision rules are activated in force but not ratified. Their item list was published mid-batch, so every number off it carries item set self-enumerated until a verification pass clears it. 4 open

The first two entries are a claim about authorship order, and that is the point of them: a scoring scheme fixed after someone has seen a result cannot be shown not to have been fitted to it. Those two can. The last two are the counterweight, and I would rather state them than round them off — the scheme was amended once mid-stream, four rubrics are usable today and explicitly not settled, and the outcome-key rubric has not been applied at all, because applying it is a separate blind pass and every rater on this wave is disqualified from it by construction.

Six of the thirty traces are double-coded; the other 24 are single-coded and carry single-coded — reliability-untested in the same sentence as any number derived from them. Disagreements stay in the data: the protocol has me adjudicate them as an added record, so one trace can yield two rows and any merge must key on rater as well as trace. Ten disagreements from the double-coded six are still open — none has been adjudicated yet, and that is the current blocking task, not coding.

07What ran, and what it found

Three batches, 51 traces, $26.05 for the main one — and three design defects that each changed the apparatus.

Outcomes across the main batch: 10 completed · 11 hit the turn cap · 8 quit · 1 ended with no canary. One seed tree hash across all thirty — the comparability proof held. An earlier run-cut sits in an archive and is never pooled, because an apparatus change (a container locale fix) sits between it and the rest. That change is recorded in no trace artifact, only in a derived filename and in prose; stamping container environment alongside the image digest is a fix I have specified and not yet implemented.

The project write-up tells the three defects as narrative; here is the mechanism and what each one changed.

Defect 1 — the card had no reachable right answer

Mechanism: the stale-page card asks for content that exists nowhere in the apparatus, so all fifteen traces were attempting an impossible repair; one passed a changed: yes check by stamping a fresh date over unchanged content. What changed: the launcher now copies the artifact's end state out before teardown with a post-run hash, correctness is coded rather than inferred from the changed-flag, and a later card was built with a right answer derivable from its own payload — the first one on which a functional-versus-factual prediction is scorable at all.

Defect 2 — the split tracked tool access, not attitude

Mechanism: the harness grants no shell tool and the app has no terminal, but the container desktop has a terminal launcher on its taskbar. Some conditions left the app and clicked it; others never did, and that difference — not mindset — produced the batch's cleanest-looking split. What changed: I kept the terminal and promoted leaving the product to a pre-registered measure, scored on a surface a condition actually opened (a taskbar click), never on one its narration merely mentioned, and stamped into every run manifest before the run. Prospective only — those fifteen traces stay confounded and labelled.

Defect 3 — a uniform tax on a differentially scarce budget

Mechanism: the pinned image ships no clipboard utility, so all 30 traces fell back to a slower typing path, costing a measured 6–27% of active turns to render races and re-sends. The tax is uniform across conditions; the cap binds on active turns; and the censoring is differential — one grounded condition hit the cap 5 of 5, the control 0 of 5, with the rest in between. A flat cost paid out of an unequal budget lands hardest on the conditions under study. What changed: cap-hit cells are drawn as right-censored rather than as completed trajectories, and the cap moved from a budget setting to an open pre-registration question — which is what stopped the next batch.

08Verdict, open gates, and limits

Coding is done and adjudication is not; the turn cap is unresolved; four gates stand between this and any counted claim.

No further runs on the current card

Three conditions were set for resuming. (a) all 30 traces coded — met. (b) a pre-registered rule for the probe and tension measures — met provisionally, which discharges the condition only as far as a provisional rubric discharges anything. (c) the turn cap resolved by pre-registration, not by budget — open, and sharper after coding than before it. Two card-specific reasons reinforce it: the outcome rubric exists but has not been applied, and the working seed's date window has closed, so no further run on this cut would be interpretable anyway.

Four gates stand between calibration status and a counted result. Three carry a proposed default so that silence resolves them rather than stalling them — and I am showing the proposals, including the one where my own proposal is to wave the gate through, because a sanitised gate table would be worse than useless.

Limits

  • Patterns found in data, not segments. No frequency, prevalence or population claim. A seven-person convenience corpus whose participants share an overlapping course and professional context.
  • The carriers are two steps removed from the interviews — derived from a teammate's synthesis, and partly anchored on participants whose transcripts are not in my workspace.
  • Agreement is between LLM sessions, not people. No human reliability check exists, for the trace coding or the interview coding.
  • Most results are single-coded, and say so in the same breath as the number.
  • Ten disagreements are unadjudicated, and four rubrics are in force but not ratified.
  • Nothing here is counted. 51 traces of calibration data and four open gates.
